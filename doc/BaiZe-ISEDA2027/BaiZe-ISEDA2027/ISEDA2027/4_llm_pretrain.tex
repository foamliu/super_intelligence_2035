\section{LLM Pretraining}\label{sec:llm-pretrain}

Stage~(i) pre-trains the hybrid language backbone (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) from scratch under a fixed budget.
This section covers the architecture-selection rationale, the training recipe, and the resulting measurements; the backbone configuration itself is given in Table~\ref{tab:config}.

\subsection{Architecture Selection Rationale}\label{sec:arch-rationale}
We adopt the hybrid backbone on three grounds.
\textbf{(i) Inference efficiency.} State-space layers decode with constant-time recurrence per token, while the sparse attention layers retain long-range context; this directly targets the inference-heavy, cost-sensitive deployments that motivate BaiZe.
\textbf{(ii) Controlled comparison.} A matched from-scratch comparison at the $\approx$2B scale between a dense transformer (MiniCPM5-2B) and Mamba2-hybrid---trained under identical data, seed, and GPU footprint---favours the hybrid, which converges to a lower loss with fewer parameters ($\approx$2.22B vs.\ 2.51B, $-11.6\%$) and decodes faster under our direct-drive harness, at a $\sim$24\% training-throughput cost (Table~\ref{tab:archcomp}).
\textbf{(iii) Feasibility.} Megatron-Core ships a Mamba-hybrid provider whose 56-layer interleaving pattern we reuse verbatim, halving only the hidden and FFN widths to reach the $\approx$2B scale, making the backbone directly executable under the NeMo recipe without custom kernels.

\subsection{Training Framework and Reduced-Horizon Search}\label{sec:framework}
We train with the NVIDIA NeMo pre-training entry point, driving the Megatron-Core~\cite{nvidia-megatron} \texttt{NVIDIAMambaHybridModelProvider2B} provider directly with $\texttt{tensor\_model\_parallel\_size}=1$.
To keep the entire sweep within budget, we run all hyper-parameter searches at a reduced iteration count and treat the resulting short-horizon ranking as predictive of the full-model optimum.
Fused kernels, selective activation recomputation, and cross-entropy loss fusion are enabled to raise throughput.

\subsection{Learning-Rate Schedule}\label{sec:wsd}
BaiZe trains under a warmup--stable--decay-style learning-rate schedule whose hyper-parameters---warmup length, stable learning rate, and the decay ratio---are themselves swept in Section~\ref{sec:search} (cosine decay is kept as an alternative).
The remaining schedule and batch-size settings (global batch size, sequence length, packing) are held fixed across configurations and re-measured on the final backbone, with figures reported where applicable.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Search Protocol}\label{sec:search}
Because a full grid is infeasible under a fixed GPU budget, we adopt a staged, single-variable-at-a-time protocol:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S1 (baseline).} Reproduce the Mamba2-hybrid reference configuration (stable LR $3\times10^{-4}$, WSD) to establish a loss/throughput baseline.
\item \textbf{S2 (learning-rate sweep).} Sweep the stable learning rate over seven points from $2\times10^{-4}$ to $1\times10^{-3}$ with WSD held fixed.
\item \textbf{S3 (schedule axes).} On the winning learning rate, perturb one variable at a time---schedule family (WSD vs.\ cosine), decay ratio, warmup length, and $min\_lr$.
\item \textbf{S4 (annealing data mix).} Ablate the annealing data blend (pure Ultra-FineWeb-L3 vs.\ adding code and math) under the winning configuration.
\item \textbf{S5 (long run + seeds).} Retrain the winning configuration from scratch over a $20000$-step horizon, plus a three-seed short-horizon reproducibility check.
\end{itemize}

At every stage only a single variable changes relative to the running best, isolating each factor's contribution while keeping total GPU-hours within budget.
The architectural search space is validated up front: the hybrid (Mamba2-hybrid) and dense (MiniCPM5-2B) candidates are compared head-to-head from scratch (Section~\ref{sec:arch-rationale}), which selects the hybrid backbone before the hyper-parameter search begins.

\subsection{Data}\label{sec:llm-data}
Pre-training uses the \texttt{Ultra-FineWeb-L3} English subset ($\approx$690\,B tokens across 616 parquet shards, 618\,GiB), streamed and packed to a sequence length of $4096$, tokenised with the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash tokenizer (vocabulary size $129{,}281$).
Complementary corpora---code (\texttt{UltraData-Code}, $\approx$411\,B tokens, 1.22\,TB), mathematics (\texttt{UltraData-Math}, $\approx$303\,B tokens, 552\,GB), and an agentic instruction-tuning set (\texttt{UltraData-SFT-Agent-2609}, $51$\,G)---are reserved for post-training (Section~\ref{sec:llm-posttrain}).
For the full-scale production run, the larger \texttt{Ultra-FineWeb} base corpus (English high-quality tier, $\approx$2.66\,TB / 6{,}000 parquet shards, plus a Chinese subset of 256 shards / 324\,GB) is being prepared to supersede L3 as the stable-phase main corpus; the L3 subset is retained for the annealing tail.
Contamination control is enforced before any corpus enters training: a blacklist of $193{,}295$ character-level 13-gram fingerprints (FNV-1a 64-bit hashed, NFKC-normalised) is built from all $536$ tasks across six evaluation snapshots of EDA-Eval-PyAether, and every new data shard---including SFT corpora---is scanned for ${\geq}0.80$ n-gram overlap before release (Section~\ref{sec:posttrain-eval}).

\subsection{Data Mixture Proportion Search}\label{sec:mix-search}
Because the optimal web/code/math ratio is scale-dependent and a full-scale sweep is infeasible, we searched the three-way mixture at a proxy scale using Bayesian optimisation.
A compact proxy model ($d{=}128$, $L{=}14$, $\approx$18.36\,M parameters---two orders of magnitude smaller than the 2.2\,B target and not intended as a performance predictor) was trained for each trial under Optuna's \texttt{TPESampler} with \texttt{MedianPruner}, one trial per GPU, global batch size $2048$ at sequence length $4096$.
The objective was the average accuracy on eight common-sense \texttt{lm-eval} tasks (\texttt{arc\_challenge}, \texttt{arc\_easy}, \texttt{boolq}, \texttt{hellaswag}, \texttt{openbookqa}, \texttt{piqa}, \texttt{sciq}, \texttt{winogrande}), each evaluated with \texttt{-{}-limit~500} subsampling.

Two rounds of 200 trials each were completed.
In the first round, using final training loss as the objective, the Spearman rank correlation between loss-ranked and accuracy-ranked mixtures was $\rho{=}{-}0.43$, confirming that loss is a weak proxy for downstream ability.
In the second round, using the eight-task average directly, the top-5 mixtures were retrained and re-evaluated at the full \texttt{lm-eval} scale.
Table~\ref{tab:mix-topk} reports the results: the full-eval rankings were nearly inverted relative to the Bayesian-optimisation rankings (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.80$, $n{=}5$, $p{\approx}0.10$), and the spread across all five mixtures was only $0.383$--$0.390$ ($\approx 0.6$\,pp).

\emph{At the proxy scale of 18.36\,M parameters, data-mixture rankings were indistinguishable within 0.6\,pp}, leading us to adopt the $88{:}8{:}4$ (web/code/math) prior from the literature rather than over-fitting to proxy-scale noise.
The empirically optimal region was stable at web $0.87$--$0.94$, code $0.06$--$0.12$, math $\approx$$0.01$, consistent with the prior.
Raising the discrimination floor---via a larger proxy, multiple seeds, or full-scale evaluation replacing the \texttt{-{}-limit~500} subsampling---is left to future work.

\subsection{Search Budget}\label{sec:llm-budget}
The pre-training search is bound by a $\approx$92\,GPU-hour plan on eight H100 GPUs ($\approx$192\,GPU-hours wall-clock available); $\approx$85\,GPU-hours were consumed.
All short-horizon sweeps (S1--S4) run $5000$ steps at a global batch size of $8\times4094\approx32.7$K tokens/step ($\approx$164M tokens, $\approx$4.0\,GPU-hours each); the verification long run (S5) runs $20000$ steps ($\approx$655M tokens, $\approx$13.9\,GPU-hours).
Validation loss is reported at matched step counts so that comparisons across configurations remain apples-to-apples.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:llm-results}
The winning configuration is a stable learning rate of $1\times10^{-3}$ under a warmup--stable--decay (WSD) schedule (warmup $250$ steps, decay $500$ steps, $min\_lr{=}1\times10^{-5}$), with an $86{:}10{:}4$ blend of Ultra-FineWeb-L3, code, and math data in the annealing tail.
Over the $5000$-step horizon the validation loss reaches $\mathbf{2.7647}$.
The learning-rate sweep improves monotonically from $2.9037$ ($2\times10^{-4}$) to $2.7627$ ($1\times10^{-3}$); because $1\times10^{-3}$ is also the grid maximum, that on its own leaves open whether the true optimum lies beyond the grid.
Extending the grid upward closes the question: $1.5\times10^{-3}$ gives $2.7752$ and $2\times10^{-3}$ gives $2.7889$, so the sweep is \emph{U-shaped} and $1\times10^{-3}$ is an interior minimum rather than a clipped boundary.
Each short-horizon ablation confirms a single-variable choice: WSD beats cosine ($2.7679$ vs.\ $2.8155$), a $10\%$ decay ratio beats $5\%$ ($2.7679$ vs.\ $2.7900$), a $250$-step ($5\%$) warmup beats a fixed $2000$-step warmup ($2.7679$ vs.\ $2.7957$), and $min\_lr{=}1\times10^{-5}$ narrowly beats $3\times10^{-5}$ ($2.7647$ vs.\ $2.7679$).
Adding code and math to the annealing mix lowers the final loss monotonically ($2.7621$ pure L3 $\to$ $2.6793$ L3+code $\to$ $2.6298$ L3+code+math).

The $20000$-step verification run reaches a final training loss of $\mathbf{2.2054}$, with the decay tail (steps $19500$--$20000$) annealing the loss from $\sim2.34$ to $2.2054$ (annealing gain $\sim0.14$; Figure~\ref{fig:pretrain-curve}).
Across three seeds the $5000$-step loss is $2.6739\pm0.0469$ (mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation, $n{=}3$; per-seed $2.6402$, $2.6539$, $2.7275$).
