\section{Vision Encoder Pretraining}\label{sec:vision}

Stage~(iii) pre-trains a custom vision encoder from scratch for the multimodal stage (Section~\ref{sec:mllm}).
This section presents the architecture-selection rationale, the pre-training recipe, and the resulting measurements.

\subsection{Architecture Selection}\label{sec:vis-rationale}
We compare six candidate vision encoders at an iso-parameter $500$--$600$\,M scale, all \emph{randomly initialized} (no upstream ImageNet/CLIP/SigLIP weights) so that only the architecture body is contrasted.
All candidates are trained under a unified stack: \texttt{open\_clip}~3.2.0 loss modules with a \emph{frozen} CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 text tower ($768$-d, context $77$), the CLIP InfoNCE (softmax-contrastive) objective, and the Stanford GPIC \texttt{short} caption split ($\approx2.77$\,M pairs, $0\%$ truncation), at resolution~$224$ / patch~$16$ / bf16, learning rate $3{\times}10^{-3}$ (AdamW, warmup~$20$), seed~$1234$, batch $64{\times}8$ (i.e.\ $512$ in-batch negatives).
The six candidates are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{OpenVision2} --- a pure-attention ViT (width~$1024$, depth~$30$), $505.2$\,M.
\item \textbf{MambaEye} --- a pure visual state-space model (Vim-style bidirectional SSM), $535.2$\,M.
\item \textbf{MoE-ViE} --- a sparse Mixture-of-Experts encoder ($222.2$\,M active per token), $505.5$\,M total.
\item \textbf{DeepEncoderV2} --- an Attention+SSM hybrid, $517.2$\,M.
\item \textbf{AIMv2} --- a ViT-GELU variant, $505.5$\,M.
\item \textbf{FastViTHD} --- a conv-stem $+$ hierarchical conv/attention hybrid, $507.8$\,M.
\end{itemize}

The last two are \emph{iso-parameter adaptations} of the AIMv2 and FastViTHD designs to this scale, not the official released models.
Table~\ref{tab:visarch} reports the measured parameter counts, the training loss at $3000$ steps, training throughput, and---crucially---two downstream metrics on ImageNet-1k measured on the \emph{frozen} trunk: zero-shot top-1/top-5 and linear-probe top-1.

\textbf{Finding.} \textbf{OpenVision2} ranks first on every quality metric---lowest loss, most dispersed features, and the best ImageNet-1k zero-shot and linear-probe accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:visarch}).
The pure-attention family (plus MoE or a conv stem) trains normally: AIMv2 is the fastest to train but does not convert speed into quality, and neither hybrid beats a plain attention ViT at equal parameter count.

\textbf{An architecture-specific failure mode.} The two candidates that contain state-space sub-structure---MambaEye (pure SSM) and DeepEncoderV2 (attn$+$SSM)---\emph{exactly} collapse: their loss pins at $6.25\approx\ln 512$, the entropy of an unlearned $512$-way InfoNCE problem, with a same-tower feature cosine of $1.0000$ and ImageNet-1k at exactly chance ($0.10\%$).
The text tower, data, objective, batch size and seed are identical across the six runs, so the only variable is the vision architecture; under this contrastive recipe the collapse is tied to the SSM sub-structure rather than to the recipe.
We report it as a negative result, with its boundary stated: an earlier sweep under a different objective trained MambaEye without collapsing, so this is a property of the (architecture, objective) pair rather than evidence that state-space vision encoders are unusable.

\textit{Methodological note.} An earlier sweep at $lr{=}10^{-3}$ pinned every ablation cell at an identical loss, because the cosine schedule collapsed the learning rate to $\approx10^{-5}$ before the run ended.
We therefore re-ran all configurations at $lr{=}3\times10^{-3}$; sub-$1$k-step comparisons, and any $3000$-step comparison at $lr{=}10^{-3}$, are uninformative, and every number in this section uses the corrected rate.

\subsection{Scaling Behaviour}\label{sec:vis-scaling}
Holding the winning architecture fixed, we ask how far the representation improves with data, and how tower width trades against data at a fixed sample budget.
Stage-1 trains three widths for $30000$ steps on the same $\approx18.5$\,M-pair corpus (CC12M $+$ Amshaker) under the recipe above: linear-probe top-1 falls monotonically with width---$6.10\%$ (w512, $126.8$\,M), $3.63\%$ (w768, $284.5$\,M), $1.03\%$ (w1024, $505.0$\,M).
At this data scale the \emph{smaller} tower is therefore the more sample-efficient one.
Stage-2 then trains w512 for $108{,}000$ steps ($55.3$\,M samples) and evaluates every $10{,}000$-step checkpoint on the frozen trunk (Table~\ref{tab:visscale}): linear-probe top-1 rises from $3.96\%$ at $5.1$\,M samples to a peak of $7.70\%$ at $51.2$\,M.

A power law $acc = 0.251 - 0.864\,N^{-0.090}$ fits the eleven checkpoints ($R^2\approx0.94$) and implies an asymptote of $25.1\%$ \emph{for the pure contrastive (InfoNCE) objective}: even with unlimited data this from-scratch contrastive route saturates near $25\%$ linear-probe top-1, and reaching $20\%$ would take on the order of $6\times10^{10}$ samples under the more favourable log-linear extrapolation---roughly three orders of magnitude beyond the $\approx1.2\times10^{8}$ unique image--text pairs available locally.
A complementary five-point model-size sweep (widths $384$--$1024$ at matched samples) finds the marginal effect of width to be negative throughout ($\approx-2.2$ to $-2.4$ linear-probe points per doubling of parameters) with no saturation point: the best configuration observed is the smallest tower tried ($71.5$\,M, $7.99\%$ at $15.4$\,M samples).
We initially read this as the central limitation of the vision stage---the binding constraint is data volume and objective function, not architecture.
The next subsection revisits that reading: the ceiling is specific to the contrastive objective, and switching to a caption-\emph{independent} dense objective while retaining the contrastive term breaks it.
\subsection{Breaking the Contrastive Ceiling with Dense Supervision}\label{sec:vis-dense}

The $25.1\%$ asymptote above is tied to a single global scalar signal per image--text pair (the InfoNCE loss).
A body of recent work argues that \emph{dense per-patch} supervision can be more sample-efficient than global contrastive learning: AIMv2~\cite{aimv2} reports ``denser supervision compared to discriminative objectives'' from a masked-patch generative head, SigLIP~2~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2} augments sigmoid contrastive loss with captioning and masked-prediction heads, and OpenVision~2~\cite{openvision2} removes the text encoder entirely in favour of a purely generative objective.
Motivated by these findings, we test whether augmenting the contrastive loss with a caption-\emph{independent} dense term can break the $25.1\%$ ceiling without additional data.

\paragraph{AIMv2-style objective.}
We add a masked-patch reconstruction head to the selected tower: $60\%$ of patches are masked and their raw pixels are reconstructed by a lightweight predictor ($0.66$\,M params) via an MSE loss, while the InfoNCE contrastive term is retained at equal weight ($\lambda_{\text{patch}}=1.0$).
The dense term is \emph{caption-independent}---it supervises raw pixels, not text tokens---so it does not depend on caption quality or truncation.
This is a self-developed adaptation of the AIMv2 design philosophy, \emph{not} an official reproduction: the official AIMv2~\cite{aimv2} uses prefix-LM attention, $\alpha\approx0.4$ loss weighting, and $\sim12$\,B training pairs, none of which we replicate here.

\paragraph{Result: the ceiling is broken.}
Under the \emph{identical} architecture (OpenVision2, w512), data ($\approx18.5$\,M-pair corpus, CC12M $+$ Amshaker), and optimisation schedule as the scaling study above, the AIMv2-style objective produces a monotonically increasing linear-probe trajectory that exceeds the contrastive baseline at every checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:visaimv2}):
lp~top-1 rises from $11.50\%$ at $5.1$\,M samples to $\mathbf{19.76\%}$ at $55.3$\,M---already above the $7.40\%$ contrastive baseline at the same sample budget by $+12.4$\,pp, and the gap \emph{widens} monotonically from $+7.5$\,pp at $10.2$\,M to $+12.4$\,pp at $55.3$\,M.
A power-law fit $acc = a - b\,N^{-c}$ yields $R^2 = 0.91$; the exponent $c = 0.040$ is much smaller than the contrastive-only fit ($c = 0.090$), so the curve saturates far more slowly and no asymptote can be identified within the observed range.
A subsequent extension to $176$\,M samples ($3$ epochs over the full $58.8$\,M-pair corpus, $344$\,k steps) reaches $20.27\%$, still rising with no saturation signal; a $35$-checkpoint refit gives $R^2 = 0.94$, but the power-law asymptote hits its upper bound ($a\!\to\!1.0$) and the trajectory is non-monotonic ($7$ of $34$ checkpoints decrease, attributable to resume transients), so the precise ceiling remains indeterminate.

\paragraph{Causal decomposition: both terms are necessary.}
A four-arm ablation at $15.4$\,M samples localises the effect:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm~(i)} InfoNCE baseline: $6.08\%$.
\item \textbf{Arm~(iv)} CoCa (contrastive $+$ \emph{caption-dependent} generative CE): $0.47\%$---the caption-CE term collapses the frozen trunk, confirming that caption-\emph{dependent} generative supervision is harmful in this regime.
\item \textbf{Arm~(vi-B)} AIMv2-style without the contrastive term (pure masked-patch AR): $6.23\%$ ($+0.15$\,pp over baseline---no breakthrough).
\item \textbf{Arm~(vi-A)} AIMv2-style with both terms: $12.08\%$ ($+6.00$\,pp---breakthrough).
\end{itemize}
The breakthrough therefore depends on the \emph{interaction} of the contrastive anchor and the caption-independent dense term: neither alone suffices.
Removing the contrastive term (arm~vi-B) eliminates the breakthrough, and replacing the caption-independent term with a caption-dependent one (arm~iv) actively collapses the representation.
This is consistent with MAE~\cite{he2022mae}, where pure masked-image modelling yields lower linear-probe accuracy than contrastive methods, and with AIMv2~\cite{aimv2} and SigLIP~2~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}, which retain a contrastive or sigmoid-contrastive signal alongside the dense head.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
All AIMv2-style results are from a self-developed adaptation (not the official AIMv2), trained on $\leq58.8$\,M unique pairs (vs.\ $\sim12$\,B for the official model~\cite{aimv2}).
The precise asymptote of the dense route remains indeterminate: a $35$-checkpoint scaling refit over $5$--$176$\,M samples gives $R^2 = 0.94$, but the power-law parameter hits its upper bound ($a\!\to\!1.0$) and the trajectory is non-monotonic ($7$ of $34$ checkpoints decrease, attributable to resume transients), so we report it honestly without forcing an extrapolation.
Nonetheless, $176$\,M samples show no saturation signal---the dense route reaches $20.27\%$, a level the contrastive route would need on the order of $10^{10}$ samples to attain---confirming that the $25.1\%$ ceiling is specific to the contrastive objective.
The full-data mix (GPIC $+$ CC12M $+$ Amshaker, $58.8$\,M pairs) is less sample-efficient at matched $N$ than the CC12M$+$Amshaker-only corpus ($16.80\%$ vs.\ $19.76\%$ at $\approx55$\,M samples), likely because GPIC long and tag captions are truncated at the $77$-token context; the caption-\emph{independent} patch-MSE term is unaffected, and the dense route still exceeds the contrastive baseline by a wide margin ($+9.4$ to $+12.4$\,pp at $\approx55$\,M samples, depending on data mix).
The four-arm decomposition is conducted at a single sample budget ($15.4$\,M); the interaction effect (both terms necessary) is confirmed at larger scale, where the full AIMv2-style objective continues to exceed the contrastive baseline by a wide margin through $176$\,M samples, while removing the contrastive term (arm~vi-B) yields no breakthrough at any scale tested.

\paragraph{Mask-ratio ablation.}
The dense patch-MSE term in the AIMv2-style objective masks a fraction of patches and reconstructs their raw pixels.
We sweep the mask ratio $\{0.3, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9\}$ at $30000$ steps on the same w512 / CC12M$+$Amshaker / InfoNCE$+$patch-MSE (1:1) recipe, with the ratio $0.6$ serving as the baseline (it reproduces the arm~vi-A setting).
Table~\ref{tab:vismask} shows an inverted-U: $0.6$ is optimal ($13.49\%$), and both lower and higher ratios are worse, with the steeper falloff on the high side ($0.9 \to 11.53\%$, $\Delta{=}{-}1.96$\,pp).
None of the alternatives exceeds the $+1.5$\,pp pre-registered threshold, so $0.6$ is retained.
The inverted-U is consistent with the MAE literature: too little masking starves the reconstruction signal, while too much masking leaves the predictor with insufficient context.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Domain task metrics are not yet available for this stage, so we adopt the standard frozen-trunk protocol: ImageNet-1k zero-shot (class prototypes synthesised from the frozen CLIP text tower) and a linear probe on the frozen trunk, over a deterministic self-split of the ImageNet-1k training set ($50$ images per class for the probe and $50$ for validation, disjoint from every corpus used for training).
We first tried a zero-shot text$\leftrightarrow$image retrieval proxy ($R@1/5/10$ on a held-out 5K subset of \texttt{laioncn/EN}, disjoint from the training source) and found it \emph{degenerate}: $R@1{=}0.0002$, exactly chance for $5000$ candidates, separating neither learning rates nor architectures.
We report that as a methodological warning and use ImageNet-1k instead, on which the architectures do separate---healthy candidates reach $2.5$--$9.5\times$ chance, whereas the two collapsed ones sit at exactly chance.
Resolution is not a free variable in this comparison: $224/16$ is best on both loss and throughput (Table~\ref{tab:visres}).
Stage~(iv) revisits the visual-token count for detail-dense layout and schematic views, where the reasoning of this section---that a $196$-token view is dominated by coarse semantics---does not transfer.

\subsection{Causal Autoregressive Paradigm Exploration}\label{sec:vis-ar}

The AIMv2-style objective above uses a \emph{bidirectional} vision trunk with random masking, which differs from the official AIMv2~\cite{aimv2}, where the vision encoder uses \emph{causal} (prefix-LM) attention and the objective is a pure autoregressive (AR) next-patch prediction plus a text-AR caption loss, with \emph{no} contrastive term.
The official model trains on $\sim12$\,B image--text pairs with long LLaMA-3 synthetic captions.
We test whether the pure-AR paradigm is viable at our data scale ($\approx7.7$\,M pairs, short alt-text captions) and whether the absence of a contrastive term causes the collapse observed in earlier experiments.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Three arms are compared on the same OpenVision2 w512 architecture, data (CC12M$+$Amshaker), and $30000$-step schedule:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm~A} (baseline): bidirectional attention $+$ InfoNCE $+$ masked-patch-MSE ($\lambda_{\text{patch}}{=}1.0$, mask~$0.6$) $\to$ lp${=}13.49\%$.
\item \textbf{Arm~B} (official-style pure AR): causal (prefix-LM) attention $+$ caption-AR loss $+$ $0.4{\times}$pixel loss, \emph{no} InfoNCE.
\item \textbf{Arm~B-hybrid}: causal attention $+$ $1.0{\times}$InfoNCE $+$ caption-AR loss $+$ $0.4{\times}$pixel loss.
\end{itemize}
We monitor a collapse diagnostic $C_1$ (same-tower off-diagonal feature cosine; $C_1{\to}1$ means all features collapse to a single direction) and a gap guard $C_2$ (diagonal vs.\ off-diagonal attention entropy).

\paragraph{Result: pure AR collapses, hybrid survives but underperforms.}
Arm~B collapses within $600$ steps: $C_1$ rises from $0.86$ (step~300) to $0.97$ (step~600), triggering the circuit-breaker at $2\%$ of training.
The loss is still decreasing (caption loss $6.77 \to 5.08$), so the collapse is a representation failure, not an optimisation failure---the AR next-patch task admits a trivial solution that collapses all features.
This contrasts with a bidirectional model trained \emph{without} contrastive loss ($C_1{\approx}0.43$, no collapse), showing that \emph{causal} attention is more collapse-prone than bidirectional when the contrastive anchor is removed.

Adding the InfoNCE term (Arm~B-hybrid) prevents collapse entirely: $C_1$ oscillates between $0.30$ and $0.55$ throughout $30000$ steps, with a positive $C_2$ gap and no fuse trigger.
However, the linear-probe accuracy is dramatically lower: lp${=}1.37\%$ at step~$30000$, compared with $13.49\%$ for the bidirectional baseline---a drop of $12.1$\,pp.
Zero-shot accuracy is essentially at chance ($0.37\%$ vs.\ $0.10\%$ for $1000$ classes).
The causal constraint---each patch attends only to its prefix---restricts information flow, and at our data scale ($\sim7.7$\,M pairs with short captions) this restriction severely degrades representation quality.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Two findings emerge:
(i)~The contrastive term is \emph{necessary} to prevent collapse in the causal AR paradigm at our data scale: without it, the AR objective admits a trivial collapse solution; with it, features remain dispersed.
(ii)~The causal AR paradigm \emph{cannot match} the bidirectional paradigm's representation quality at our scale, even with the contrastive anchor: the prefix-only attention constraint limits each patch to its causal context, and the resulting representations are much poorer for linear probing.
The official AIMv2 avoids this at $\sim12$\,B pairs with long LLaMA-3 captions that provide a strong text-AR signal; our $\sim7.7$\,M short-caption pairs cannot sustain the causal AR route.
We therefore retain the bidirectional $+$ InfoNCE $+$ masked-patch-MSE recipe as the final configuration.
