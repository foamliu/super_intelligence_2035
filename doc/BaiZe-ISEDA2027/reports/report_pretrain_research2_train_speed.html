Research Report 2: Opportunities to Improve Hybrid-2B Training Speed

BaiZe Mamba2-Hybrid 2.220B (4/56 attn) · NeMo Framework · 8×H100 80GB NV18

bf16 baseline: 249,040 tok/s (MFU 60.6%) · FP8 delayed: validated but net slower · Generated: 2026-10-07

Evidence grading: 【实测·本项目】 【一手文献·URL】 【二手·未核】 【推测·未验】

1. TL;DR

Our 2.22B hybrid trains at 249K tok/s (MFU 60.6%) on 8×H100. The #1 bottleneck is communication (41.7% of device time), not compute. NCCL is already on NVLink P2P (optimal), all overlap flags are on, and env-variable sweeps yield 0% improvement. The only remaining speedup lever is FP8 — but our FP8 tests show a paradox: FP8 speeds up GEMM by 24%, yet requires TP4 (which adds comm overhead), resulting in net 6% slower throughput. The path forward is FP8 on TP1 (untested) or code-level kernel/communication optimization.

  1. Communication is the #1 bottleneck, not compute 【实测·本项目】: P-9.5 profiler (torch.profiler, 5-way attribution) shows comm=41.7%, GEMM=27.6%, elementwise=21.3%, SSM=8.6%, attention=0.7%. The hybrid architecture's 4/56 attention design makes attention negligible (0.7%) — a direct quantification of the architecture's training advantage.
  2. NCCL is already optimal — no env-variable speedup available 【实测·本项目】: P-9.12 confirmed NV18 full-mesh NVSwitch, 100% P2P/CUMEM, busbw 164.56 GB/s (68.8% of 478 GB/s peak). P-9.13 swept MAX_CONN∈{1,4} (+0–0.8%, noise) and MBS=4 (OOM at 79.8GB). 249K tok/s is the launcher-exposed parameter ceiling.
  3. FP8 validated for accuracy but paradoxically slower in throughput 【实测·本项目】: P-9.8 confirmed delayed FP8 passes all 4 long-run criteria (1000 steps, loss consistency ✅, nan=0 ✅, s=1.194 ✅, accuracy ≤2% ✅). P-9.9 confirmed tensorwise FP8 FAILs (persistent +6–7% loss divergence). But FP8 requires TP4·MBS8·seq8192 (M=65536) to turn on (s=1.24), and TP4's communication overhead (−7.8pp MFU) eats the GEMM speedup → net 235K vs 249K bf16 (−6%).
  4. All recipe-level optimizations are already enabled 【实测·本项目】: Recipe audit (P-9.13) confirms overlap_grad_reduce=True, overlap_param_gather=True, use_distributed_optimizer=True, num_workers=8. Launcher exposes only 23 flags — no recompute, cuda_graph, attention_backend, or loss_fusion entry points. Further speedup requires code-level changes or FP8-on-TP1 testing.
  5. The MBS=1→2 lesson: grad-accum bubbles were the low-hanging fruit 【实测·本项目】: P-9.1 showed MBS 1→2 = +59% throughput (MFU 33%→61%, grad-accum segments 128→64). This is already captured. MBS=4 OOMs at 79.8GB on TP1 — the memory wall prevents further bubble reduction without activation checkpointing.

3. External Evidence (First-Hand Sources)

3.1 Mamba2 SSD: Chunked Parallel Scan 【一手文献·URL】

Paper: Dao & Gu, "Transformers are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality," arXiv 2405.21060, May 2024

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060

Key claims:

  • Mamba2 replaces the custom parallel scan kernel of Mamba1 with a chunked matrix multiplication formulation (SSD), enabling native tensor-parallel support and better GPU utilization
  • Mamba2 core layer is 2–8× faster than Mamba1 on A100
  • The SSD formulation allows the computation to be expressed as either recurrence (efficient inference) or matmul (efficient training)

Relevance: Our 8.6% SSM time confirms the Mamba2 kernel is already efficient. The chunked scan formulation is what we're using (mamba-ssm 2.2.6). Further SSM speedup would require custom kernel work beyond the stock implementation.

3.2 FP8 Training: Throughput Gains and Recipes 【一手文献·URL】

Source 1: NVIDIA Transformer Engine documentation, FP8 recipes

URL: https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/transformer-engine-user-guide/index.html (accessed 2026-10-07)

Key mechanism:

  • Delayed scaling: amax from history buffer (past N steps) → one global memory read, good performance, but accuracy risk at >7B scale 【一手文献·URL】
  • Current (per-tensor) scaling: amax from current tensor → two reads, slightly slower, better accuracy (used by Nemotron-H-56B)
  • Blockwise scaling (128×128 tiles): best accuracy, requires CUDA ≥ 12.9
  • FP8 Tensor Core: 2× peak FLOP/s vs bf16 on H100 (1979 vs 989 TFLOP/s)

Source 2: Emergent Mind FP8 training survey (2025)

URL: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/fp8-training-on-trillion-token-datasets (accessed 2026-10-07)

Reported throughput gains (vs BF16): 25–40% (μnit Scaling 25–33%, FOG 18–40%, COAT 1.43×, TWEO 36%). 【二手·未核】 for exact figures — aggregate from multiple papers.

Relevance: Our P-9.8 measured s=1.194 (19.4%) for delayed FP8 — within the 18–40% range, but lower than typical because our comm fraction (41.7%) is higher than pure-compute models. The literature's 25–40% assumes comm is a smaller fraction.

3.3 Nemotron-H: Production FP8 Hybrid 【一手文献·URL】

Paper: NVIDIA, "Nemotron-H: A Family of Accurate and Efficient Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Models," arXiv 2504.03624, April 2025 (v4 Sep 2025)

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624

Key claims:

  • 8B model: 52 layers, 4 attention (8%) — same 4-attn design as BaiZe
  • FP8 training recipe achieves on-par accuracy with BF16
  • Up to 3× faster inference vs same-size Transformers
  • Uses per-tensor current scaling for the 56B model (delayed scaling has convergence issues at >7B)

Relevance: Nemotron-H validates the 4-attn hybrid design at 8B scale with FP8. Our 2.2B model uses delayed scaling (P-9.8 validated), but at P-8's 100B token scale, we should monitor for convergence drift. Nemotron-H's choice of current scaling at 56B aligns with our P-9.9 finding that tensorwise (current) has accuracy issues at 2.2B — suggesting our scale is below the delayed→current crossover.

3.4 Megatron-Core: Optimization Features 【一手文献·URL】

Source: DeepWiki Megatron-LM Optimization and Performance

URL: https://deepwiki.com/dialectics67/Megatron-LM/7-optimization-and-performance (accessed 2026-10-07)

Key optimization features (Megatron-Core 0.16.1, our version):

  • FP8: 3–5× speed, 75% memory savings (vs 2–3× for FP16/BF16), requires Transformer Engine
  • Activation checkpointing (recompute): trades compute for memory, enables larger MBS
  • CUDA Graph capture: eliminates kernel launch overhead for repetitive iteration patterns
  • Communication overlap: overlap_grad_reduce + overlap_param_gather + distributed_optimizer (all already enabled in our recipe)
  • Context parallelism: ring-attention for long sequences, reduces KV memory 40%

    • 4. Candidate List — Speedup Opportunities

    C1. FP8 delayed on TP1·MBS2·seq4096 (untested configuration) P0 · Critical

    ① Motivation: Our FP8 tests (P-9.6②, P-9.8) used TP4·MBS8·seq8192 (M=65536) because FP8 only "turns on" (s>1.05) at large M. But TP4 adds −7.8pp MFU comm overhead, eating the GEMM speedup → net −6% throughput. FP8 on TP1 (our optimal comm config) has never been tested. P-9.4 tested TP2·MBS4·seq4096 and got s≤1.01 (no speedup) — but that was TP2, not TP1. 【实测·本项目】
    ② Expected benefit: If FP8 turns on at TP1·MBS2·seq4096 (M=8192), we get 19% GEMM speedup WITHOUT the TP4 comm penalty → potential 249K × 1.19 × (1−0.417×0.19/0.583) ≈ 278K tok/s (+12%). If FP8 doesn't turn on (like TP2), we learn the M threshold for FP8 activation. 【推测·未验】
    ③ Cost/risk: Low. ~1h GPU (60-step short test, same as P-9.6②). Risk: FP8 at M=8192 may be memory-bandwidth-bound (P-9.4 showed s≤1.01 at M=16384 TP2) → no speedup. But TP1 has different memory access patterns than TP2.
    ④ Testable criteria: Run TP1·DP8·MBS2·seq4096·FP8_delayed, 60 steps. Measure s/iter vs bf16 baseline (16,841.9 ms). s ≥ 1.10 → FP8 viable on TP1 → re-evaluate for P-8. s < 1.05 → FP8 requires large M, TP1 not viable.
    ⑤ Priority: P0 — this is the single most impactful untested experiment. If it works, it's a free 12% speedup for P-8's 100B token run (saves ~1.5 days).

    C2. Activation checkpointing (recompute) to enable MBS=4 P0 · Critical

    ① Motivation: MBS 1→2 gave +59% throughput (P-9.1). MBS=4 OOMs at 79.8GB (P-9.13). Activation checkpointing (recompute) trades ~33% more compute for ~40% memory savings → could fit MBS=4 in 80GB. 【一手文献·URL】 (Megatron-Core docs: recompute saves activations memory). 【实测·本项目】 (P-9.13: MBS4=79.8GB OOM)
    ② Expected benefit: MBS=4 halves grad-accum segments (64→32), reducing bubble overhead. If MFU scales linearly (60.6%→~70%), throughput → ~287K tok/s (+15%). But recompute adds ~20% compute overhead → net may be ~250–270K. 【推测·未验】
    ③ Cost/risk: Medium. Requires recipe/launcher modification (recompute flag not exposed — P-9.13 recipe audit). Risk: recompute + MBS4 may still OOM if activation savings < 25GB needed. Risk: recompute changes numerics slightly.
    ④ Testable criteria: Add --recompute-num-layers 28 (or selective) to recipe, run MBS=4, 60 steps. Measure: peak VRAM (<80GB?), tok/s, loss consistency (vs MBS=2 baseline). VRAM < 75GB AND tok/s > 260K → viable for P-8.
    ⑤ Priority: P0 — second most impactful. Combined with C1 (FP8 TP1), could push to 300K+ tok/s.

    C3. CUDA Graph capture for iteration kernel fusion P1 · Important

    ① Motivation: elementwise = 21.3% of device time (P-9.5). Many elementwise ops are small kernel launches with overhead. CUDA Graph captures the entire forward/backward as a single graph, eliminating per-kernel launch overhead. 【一手文献·URL】 (Megatron-Core supports CUDA Graph; PyTorch torch.cuda.CUDAGraph)
    ② Expected benefit: Eliminates kernel launch overhead for the 21.3% elementwise + 8.6% SSM ops. Estimated 5–15% speedup on the compute portion → ~3–8% end-to-end. 【推测·未验】
    ③ Cost/risk: Medium-high. Requires launcher/recipe modification (cuda_graph flag not exposed). Risk: CUDA Graph requires static shapes (our WSD schedule has fixed shapes ✅), but dynamic loss scaling or variable-length data may break capture. Risk: debugging difficulty if graph capture fails.
    ④ Testable criteria: Enable --cuda-graph in recipe (if Megatron-Core supports it for Mamba2 hybrid), run 60 steps. Measure tok/s vs baseline. tok/s > 260K → viable.
    ⑤ Priority: P1 — promising but requires code changes and may not be compatible with Mamba2 SSM scan (dynamic sequence-dependent shapes).

Relevance: Our launcher (23 flags) does NOT expose recompute, cuda_graph, or context_parallel. These are available in Megatron-Core 0.16.1 but require recipe/launcher modification. This is the code-level optimization path.

C4. Context parallelism for longer training sequences P1 · Important

① Motivation: If P-8 uses seq > 4096 (for long-context capability), TP-based sequence parallelism adds comm overhead (P-9.6①: TP4 = −7.8pp MFU). Context parallelism (ring-attention) distributes the attention computation across GPUs with O(seq_len) communication, reducing KV memory 40%. 【一手文献·URL】 (Megatron-Core context parallelism, NVIDIA docs)
② Expected benefit: For the 4 attention layers only (0.7% of time), CP may not help much. But if P-8 targets seq8192+, CP avoids the TP4 penalty. The SSM layers (8.6%) are inherently O(n) and don't need CP. 【推测·未验】
③ Cost/risk: Medium. Requires recipe modification. Risk: CP only helps attention layers; with 4/56 attention, the benefit is marginal. Risk: CP + Mamba2 interaction untested.
④ Testable criteria: Compare TP4·SP (current long-ctx path) vs CP at seq8192. Measure tok/s and comm fraction. CP tok/s > TP4 tok/s (235K) → viable alternative for long-ctx P-8.
⑤ Priority: P1 — only relevant if P-8 uses seq > 4096. For pure throughput at seq4096, not applicable.

C5. Upgrade Transformer Engine / Megatron-Core P1 · Important

① Motivation: Current: TE 2.12.0, mcore 0.16.1, CUDA 12.8. Megatron-Core 0.11+ (NeMo 25.02) introduces hierarchical AllReduce, chunked inter-DC comms, and improved FP8 kernels. 【一手文献·URL】 (NVIDIA blog: NeMo 25.02 + mcore 0.11.0, May 2025). Blockwise FP8 (128×128) requires CUDA ≥ 12.9 (P-9.9: rc=1 on CUDA 12.8). 【实测·本项目】
② Expected benefit: Blockwise FP8 may give better accuracy than delayed (P-9.9 tensorwise failed, but blockwise is a different recipe). Updated TE kernels may have better kernel fusion. 【推测·未验】
③ Cost/risk: High. Upgrading CUDA/TE/mcore mid-project risks breaking the validated P-5b recipe. Risk: new bugs, regression in FP8 delayed behavior. Must be done BEFORE P-8 starts, not during.
④ Testable criteria: Upgrade CUDA to 12.9+, TE to latest, test blockwise FP8 128×128. Run P-9.8-equivalent 1000-step consistency check. All 4 criteria pass → upgrade viable.
⑤ Priority: P1 — high potential but high risk. Only if P-8 hasn't started and upgrade window is available.

C6. Fused SSM + elementwise kernel (custom CUDA) P2 · Exploratory

① Motivation: SSM (8.6%) + elementwise (21.3%) = 29.9% of device time. Many elementwise ops (norms, activations, residuals) could be fused with the SSM scan kernel. 【一手文献·URL】 (Mamba2 SSD chunked formulation allows fusion, Dao & Gu 2024)
② Expected benefit: Fusing 5–10% of elementwise into SSM → 2–3% end-to-end speedup. 【推测·未验】
③ Cost/risk: Very high. Requires custom CUDA kernel development (weeks of engineering). Risk: may break numerical equivalence with stock mamba-ssm. Not feasible within P-8 timeline.
④ Testable criteria: Profile which elementwise ops are adjacent to SSM scan. Prototype fusion for 1–2 ops. Measure kernel time reduction.
⑤ Priority: P2 — research-grade, not actionable for P-8. Document for future work.

C7. Gradient accumulation in FP8 (comm compression) P2 · Exploratory

① Motivation: comm = 41.7% is the #1 bottleneck. If gradient allreduce uses FP8 instead of FP32, communication volume halves → ~20% comm time reduction → ~8% end-to-end. 【一手文献·URL】 (FP8 communication: "no-reduction communication theoretically can use FP8" — qingkeai FP8 survey 2025)
② Expected benefit: 41.7% × 50% reduction = 20.8% → ~8.7% end-to-end speedup. 【推测·未验】
③ Cost/risk: High. FP8 allreduce requires NCCL FP8 support (available in NCCL 2.18+). Risk: FP8 gradient reduction may cause accuracy drift. Our recipe uses grad_reduce_in_fp32=True (conservative). Risk: TE/mcore may not support FP8 grad reduce for Mamba2.
④ Testable criteria: Set grad_reduce_in_fp32=False + FP8 comm, run 1000 steps. Compare loss curve to bf16 baseline. Loss Δ ≤ 2% → viable.
⑤ Priority: P2 — theoretically the highest-impact optimization (targets the #1 bottleneck directly), but high accuracy risk and untested in our stack.
