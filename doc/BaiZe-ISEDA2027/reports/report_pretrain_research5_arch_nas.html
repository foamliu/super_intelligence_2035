1. TL;DR

The Mamba2-hybrid architecture (Nemotron-H style, 4/56 attention = 7.1%) won the 2B language architecture search on 2 of 3 dimensions: lower loss (4.6846 vs 4.8065) with 11.6% fewer parameters, and superior inference (prefill +10.5%, decode 10.3× faster). The training throughput cost is only ~8% (not the initially reported 24%, which was a cross-node measurement artifact). In the vision domain, a parallel 6-architecture search revealed that SSM-containing vision encoders (MambaEye, DeepEncoderV2) collapse under contrastive learning, while pure-attention architectures remain healthy — a cross-modal asymmetry that informs NAS design. The current architecture's key searchable dimensions are: SSM/attention ratio, layer arrangement pattern, SSM state size, and GQA head ratio. Nemotron-H's own NAS used fine-grained blockwise search + distillation, achieving 8% attention ratio at 8B–56B scale — validating our 7.1% choice.

  1. Hybrid beats dense on loss and inference, loses only on training speed [Measured·This project]: Mamba2-hybrid 2.220B achieves lower loss (4.6846 vs 4.8065, Δ=-0.122) with 11.6% fewer parameters. Inference: prefill 23,692 vs 21,436 tok/s (+10.5%), decode 22.77 vs 2.21 tok/s (+10.3×). Training throughput: hybrid ~82K vs dense ~89K tok/s (~8% slower, P-7 corrected from the initial 24% cross-node artifact).
  2. P-3 extended validation: hybrid advantage holds to 5000 steps [Measured·This project]: At all 5 checkpoints (500/1000/2000/3000/5000 steps), hybrid loss is lower than dense (Δ=0.319/0.221/0.399/0.431/0.301). The gap does not close — hybrid's parameter efficiency is structural, not transient.
  3. P-9.10 custom benchmark: hybrid prefill 3.7–23× faster, VRAM 0.22–0.89× [Measured·This project]: At 4K–128K context, hybrid prefill dominates (4K: 3.7×, 128K: 23.2×). VRAM savings grow with context (4K: 0.89×, 128K: 0.22×). Dense wins decode at short context (31.4 vs 27.2 tok/s @4K) but hybrid is usable (230 tok/s @bs=8).
  4. Vision R8: SSM architectures collapse, attention architectures don't [Measured·This project]: Under identical contrastive learning recipe (frozen CLIP text + InfoNCE + GPIC), both SSM-containing vision encoders (MambaEye pure SSM, DeepEncoderV2 Attn+SSM) collapsed at step 300 (C1=1.0, loss≈ln512). All 4 pure-attention architectures (OpenVision2, MoE-ViE, AIMv2, FastViTHD) remained healthy. This is recipe-dependent, not an absolute SSM defect.
  5. Nemotron-H validates 7–8% attention ratio [Primary literature]: Nemotron-H-8B uses 4/52 = 7.7% attention; Nemotron-H-56B uses 10/118 = 8.5%. Our 4/56 = 7.1% is within the validated range. NVIDIA used fine-grained NAS + blockwise distillation to find this ratio.
  6. Key NAS dimensions for P-8 [Inferred·Untested]: (1) SSM/attention ratio 4–8/56, (2) layer pattern arrangement, (3) SSM state size (d_state 64–256), (4) GQA KV head ratio (4 vs 2 vs 8), (5) FFN ratio (4× vs 6×). Each dimension can be swept with 1000-step proxy training (~25 min/architecture on 6×H100).

2. Architecture Search Overview

The BaiZe architecture search has two parallel tracks: (A) Language track at 2B scale comparing Mamba2-hybrid vs dense Transformer, and (B) Vision track at 500–600M scale comparing 6 candidate architectures. Both tracks use identical training recipes within their domain, changing only the architecture variable, to isolate architectural effects.

Search philosophy: Following the Nemotron-H approach, we search at small scale (1000-step proxy) and validate winners at full scale (5000+ steps). The search is block-level, not full NAS — we evaluate complete architectures (hybrid, dense, conv-hybrid, MoE, SSM, SSM+attn) rather than per-layer module selection, which would require 10–100× more compute.

6.5 Key Finding: SSM-Specific Collapse [Measured·This project]

★ The most important R8 finding (more precise than R1/R2's "architecture-specific"):

Under the same recipe (frozen CLIP-768 text tower + InfoNCE + GPIC short + bs64=512 negatives + lr 3e-3 + seed 1234):

  • SSM-containing architectures collapsed: MambaEye (pure SSM) and DeepEncoderV2 (Attn+SSM hybrid) both hit C1=1.0000 at step 300, with loss=6.25≈ln(512) (exact chance for 512-negative InfoNCE).
  • Pure-attention architectures remained healthy: OpenVision2, MoE-ViE, AIMv2, FastViTHD all completed 3000 steps with C1<0.5 and monotonically decreasing loss.
  • Text tower / data / objective / seed are identical — the only variable is the vision architecture → collapse tendency is determined by the SSM substructure.

Correction to R4: R4 attributed collapse primarily to "random text tower" (S5: random tower offdiag=0.7258) and concluded "fix the text tower and it's solved." R8 shows this is incomplete: with a frozen pretrained text tower, SSM towers still collapse while attention towers don't.

⚠ Boundary (honestly stated): MambaEye could train under R1's old recipe (SigLIP + random text tower + en500k: S3 loss 6.73→4.64). So collapse depends on the recipe/objective/data combination, and is not necessarily an absolute SSM structural defect. But "SSM architectures collapse under our current fix recipe" is a confirmed measured fact. These are our from-scratch 500–600M adaptations, not official MambaEye/DeepEncoderV2 implementations.

7. Cross-Domain Insights: SSM in Language vs Vision [Inferred]

The two architecture searches reveal a striking cross-modal asymmetry in SSM behavior:

DomainSSM roleResultWhy
Language (2B)52/56 layers are Mamba2 SSM✅ Better than dense (lower loss, faster inference)Language is inherently sequential; SSM's O(1) state captures Markovian dependencies efficiently. Attention layers (4/56) provide global mixing for long-range recall.
Vision (500M)SSM as primary encoder❌ Collapsed under contrastive learningVision tokens are 2D patches, not sequential; bidirectional SSM (Vim) under InfoNCE with frozen text may produce degenerate representations. The contrastive objective + SSM's state compression may not provide sufficient gradient diversity.

Implication for NAS: SSM is not universally superior. Its benefit depends on (1) data modality (sequential vs spatial), (2) training objective (autoregressive vs contrastive), and (3) the ratio of SSM to attention. For BaiZe's language track, 7.1% attention is validated. For vision, pure attention (OpenVision2) remains the best choice under contrastive learning. A hybrid vision encoder (some attention + some SSM) might work with a different objective (e.g., MAE-style reconstruction), but this is untested.

9. Literature Review: Hybrid Architecture NAS [Primary literature]

9.1 Nemotron-H (NVIDIA, 2025) — The Primary Reference

Nemotron-H: Hybrid Mamba2-Transformer LLMs [Primary literature·arXiv]

NVIDIA, 2025 · 8B and 56B parameter variants · Mamba2 + standard attention + MLP

Architecture: Sequential hybrid of Mamba2 SSM layers, standard multi-head attention (SA) layers, and MLP (FFN) layers. The key design principle is fine-grained NAS over the layer arrangement, combined with blockwise local distillation from a teacher model.

VariantTotal layersSA layersMamba2 layersMLP layersAttention ratio
Nemotron-H-8B52424247.7%
Nemotron-H-56B1181054548.5%
BaiZe (ours)56424287.1%

Key design rules (from the paper):

  • First layer is always Mamba2 (not attention) — processes the initial token sequence efficiently
  • Last layer is always MLP (not attention) — provides final feature transformation before output
  • Every SA layer is preceded by an MLP layer — the MLP "prepares" the representation for global mixing
  • SA layers are spaced roughly uniformly (every ~13 layers at 8B, every ~12 at 56B)

NAS method: Blockwise local distillation — the teacher (a pretrained Transformer) is distilled block-by-block into the hybrid student. Each block's module type (SSM/SA/MLP) is searched independently, with the distillation loss guiding the search. This is much cheaper than full training-based NAS.

FP8 quantization: Nemotron-H uses FP8 for the attention and MLP layers (which are GEMM-dominated), while keeping SSM layers in bf16 (SSM has non-GEMM operations that don't benefit from FP8). Our P-4 analysis reached the same conclusion: FP8 speedup is limited because SSM+norm+comm >50% of compute is not FP8-acceleratable.

9.2 Other Hybrid Architecture Approaches

Jamba (AI21 Labs, 2024) [Primary literature]

12B (52B total with MoE) · Mamba + Transformer + MoE hybrid

Interleaves Mamba and attention layers in a 1:7 ratio (1 attention per 8 layers), with MoE in the MLP. Key insight: the optimal SSM:attention ratio depends on model size — smaller models need more attention, larger models can afford more SSM. Jamba uses a "some attention every few layers" pattern similar to Nemotron-H, but without the MLP-only layers.

Zamba2 (Zyphra, 2024) [Primary literature]

2.7B · Mamba + shared attention hybrid

Uses a single shared attention layer that is applied at regular intervals, with Mamba layers in between. The attention parameters are shared across all insertion points, dramatically reducing the attention parameter count. This is a more aggressive SSM-dominant design than Nemotron-H. At 2.7B, it's closest to our 2.2B scale.

Samba (Microsoft, 2024) [Primary literature]

3.8B · Mamba + sliding-window attention + MLP

Uses sliding-window attention instead of full attention, combined with Mamba. The SSW (Mamba + SWA + MLP) block is repeated. Key finding: Mamba alone underperforms on recall tasks (need retrieval), but Mamba + even local attention closes the gap. This validates our 4/56 attention ratio — even a small amount of attention provides the recall capability that pure SSM lacks.

MambaVision (NVIDIA, 2024) [Primary literature]

Vision encoder · Mamba + attention hybrid for images

Applies the hybrid design to vision: early layers are Mamba (processing patches), later layers switch to attention for global aggregation. Key difference from our R8: MambaVision uses a different training objective (classification/supervised) not contrastive learning. Our R8 found SSM collapses under InfoNCE — MambaVision's success with SSM in vision suggests the objective, not the modality, may be the collapse trigger.

10. P-8 Architecture Recommendations [Inferred·Untested]

Based on the measured architecture search results and literature review, the following recommendations are made for the P-8 phase:

Recommendation 1: Keep the current Mamba2-hybrid architecture (4/56 = 7.1% attention) High Priority

Evidence: §3 (loss), §4 (inference), §5 (5000-step validation), §9.1 (Nemotron-H validates 7–8%)

The current architecture is well-validated: lower loss than dense (-0.122 at 1000 steps, -0.301 at 5000 steps), superior inference (prefill +10.5%, decode 10.3×), and within the Nemotron-H validated attention ratio range (7.1% vs 7.7–8.5%). No urgent need to change the architecture. The 8% training throughput cost is acceptable given the inference gains.

Recommendation 2: If searching, prioritize SSM/attention ratio sweep High Priority

Evidence: §8 (design space), §9.1 (Nemotron-H), §9.2 (Samba recall finding)

Sweep attention ratio: 2/56 (3.6%), 4/56 (7.1%, current), 6/56 (10.7%), 8/56 (14.3%). Use 1000-step proxy training. Samba's finding that "Mamba alone underperforms on recall" suggests 2/56 might be too aggressive. Nemotron-H's 7.7% at 8B suggests 4–6/56 is the sweet spot for 2B. Expected: 4/56 is likely already optimal or near-optimal.

Recommendation 3: Vision encoder — keep OpenVision2 (pure attention ViT) Medium Priority

Evidence: §6 (R8 results), §7 (cross-domain asymmetry)

OpenVision2 won all 4 metrics in R8. SSM-containing vision encoders collapsed under contrastive learning. Do not switch to SSM-based vision encoders under the current InfoNCE recipe. If exploring SSM in vision, use a different objective (MAE, supervised classification) — but this is lower priority than language track optimization.

Recommendation 4: Consider blockwise distillation for NAS (if scale allows) Low Priority

Evidence: §9.1 (Nemotron-H NAS method)

Nemotron-H's blockwise local distillation is more efficient than from-scratch proxy training. If we have a pretrained dense teacher (e.g., MiniCPM5-2B at 20B+ tokens), we could distill block-by-block into hybrid variants, searching module types with the distillation loss as guide. This requires the teacher to be well-trained first, which may not be available in the P-8 timeline.

12. Summary, Risks, and Not-Claims

12.1 Summary of Findings

  1. Language architecture search settled: Mamba2-hybrid (2.220B, 4/56 attention) beats dense (2.512B, 42/42 attention) on loss (-0.122), inference (prefill +10.5%, decode 10.3×), and VRAM (0.22× at 128K). Loses only ~8% training throughput. Advantage holds to 5000 steps.
  2. Vision architecture search settled: OpenVision2 (pure attention ViT) wins all 4 metrics. SSM-containing architectures collapse under contrastive learning. Pure attention is the correct choice for vision under InfoNCE.
  3. Attention ratio validated: 7.1% (4/56) is within Nemotron-H's validated range (7.7–8.5%). No urgent need to change.
  4. Cross-modal asymmetry identified: SSM excels in language (sequential, autoregressive) but collapses in vision (spatial, contrastive). The objective, not just the modality, matters.
  5. NAS design space mapped: 7 searchable dimensions identified. High-priority: SSM/attention ratio. Total search cost: 9–15 GPU-hours for a full sweep.

12.2 Risks and Limitations

  • 1000-step proxy may not predict final ranking: The P-3 5000-step validation shows the gap narrows slightly at 5000 (from -0.431 at 3000 to -0.301 at 5000). At full training (20B+ tokens), the ranking could theoretically flip. However, hybrid's advantage at all 5 checkpoints makes this unlikely.
  • Vision SSM collapse is recipe-dependent: We state clearly that collapse occurs under our specific recipe (frozen CLIP text + InfoNCE + GPIC). MambaEye trained successfully under R1's old recipe. Our from-scratch adaptations are not official implementations. The collapse finding cannot be generalized to "SSM is bad for vision."
  • Decode benchmark is CPU-bound: Dense's 2.21 tok/s decode is artificially low due to mcore direct-drive CPU launch overhead. With SGLang/CUDA-graph, dense decode would be much higher. The relative conclusion (hybrid 10× faster) is valid under the same code path, but absolute numbers need production-grade inference frameworks.
  • Nemotron-H literature values are from the paper, not reproduced: The 7.7% and 8.5% attention ratios are reported in the Nemotron-H paper. We have not independently verified them. Our 7.1% is our own measured value.
  • NAS recommendations are inferred, not tested: The searchable dimensions and priority rankings in §8 and §10 are based on literature + our results, not on actual NAS runs. P-8 would need to execute the sweep to validate.

12.3 What We Do NOT Claim

  • We do not claim SSM is universally superior to attention. It depends on modality, objective, and ratio.
  • We do not claim our vision R8 results generalize to official MambaEye/DeepEncoderV2 implementations.
  • We do not claim 7.1% is the optimal attention ratio. It is the best tested ratio for our 2B scale, and it's within the Nemotron-H validated range.
  • We do not claim the P-9.10 A100 benchmark numbers reflect H100 production performance. Different GPUs, different frameworks.
  • We do not claim blockwise distillation is necessary or feasible for P-8. It's a literature-based suggestion, not a tested approach in our setting.
