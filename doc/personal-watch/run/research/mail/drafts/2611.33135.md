## 正文

Dear Dr. Patashnik,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents for engineering design; you can find my work at https://foamliu.github.io/.

I read BriConstrain with interest because we are exploring constrained decoding for structured physical design, where a locally valid token can still yield a globally infeasible layout. Constraining how parts attach rather than which parts attach, and the 31%→91% (BrickNet) / 15%→65% (ICLF) collision-free gains at about 60% of rejection-sampling cost, connect directly to that problem.

Three questions:

1. How is the geometric oracle precomputed, and how does its cost grow with the part catalog and placement resolution? Is the per-token check still negligible at larger catalogs?

2. When you bias attachment directions, can a globally valid build be pruned because the required orientation is locally infeasible? Do you keep a fallback for that case?

3. For ICLF numeric placements with implicit connectivity, how do you robustly recover connections from coordinates (alignment tolerance), and would the same strategy transfer to modular chiplet placement?

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
