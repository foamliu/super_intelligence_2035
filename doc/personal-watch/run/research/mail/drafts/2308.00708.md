Dear Dr. Pearce,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents; you can find my work at https://foamliu.github.io/.

I have been reading VeriGen while building an evaluation harness for Verilog generation, and your problem set plus self-written test benches changed how we score: we no longer treat textual similarity to a reference module as success. Two details in your setup matter for us, the +1.1% overall margin over GPT-3.5-turbo and the 41% gain in syntactically correct Verilog over the pretrained CodeGen-16B.

Three questions:
1. In your failure analysis, what fraction of the remaining errors are compilation failures versus simulation mismatches, and does that ratio shift between the 2B, 6B and 16B sizes?
2. You wrote the reference designs and the test benches yourselves; how did you keep the reference implementation from leaking into the bench, and did any problem get rewritten after a model passed it?
3. If you repeated the study today with a 7B-class base model, would you spend the budget on more curated Verilog (textbook versus GitHub ratio) or on sampling and reranking?

Thank you for publishing the scripts and checkpoints; they made our baseline reproducible.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
