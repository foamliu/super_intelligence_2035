## Body

Dear Dr. Howard,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents; my work is at https://foamliu.github.io/.

ULMFiT is the paper I keep returning to when we fine-tune a ~2B decoder-only model on a few tens of thousands of in-domain documents. What we actually borrowed is not the three-stage recipe as a whole, but two knobs: per-layer learning rates, and unfreezing from the top.

Two questions:

1. You set each lower layer's rate to eta_l / 2.6 and schedule with STLR at cut_frac = 0.1 and ratio = 32. On a 24-32 layer decoder-only stack, would you keep a constant 2.6 decay per layer or scale it with depth, and is there a measurable signal (say, per-layer gradient norm) that tells us the decay is too aggressive?

2. Gradual unfreezing costs one epoch per unfrozen layer. With ~30k in-domain samples, is that still a better use of compute than unfreezing everything under a single small rate, or does the advantage fade once the pretraining corpus is far larger than Wikitext-103's 103M words?

Thank you for the time.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
