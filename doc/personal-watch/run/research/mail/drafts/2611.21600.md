Dear Dr. Gandikota,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents; my work is at https://foamliu.github.io/. Your four-part account of how a VLM decides where to look — binding heads, the comma-token cursor, select, gaze heads — speaks directly to a problem we keep hitting when a 2B-scale model has to read dense technical figures.

Three questions:

1. When you replicate at 2B and 32B, are the binding and gaze heads the same layers modulo depth, or does head identity drift with scale? If it drifts, how did you align heads across sizes before causal patching?

2. How much does binding accuracy fall on natural photographs relative to your synthetic letter panels? In particular, when text is small or low-contrast, does the gaze head collapse into a "nearest unwritten region" heuristic?

3. If the same image is queried repeatedly within one conversation, is the cursor reset per answer or accumulated across turns? This decides whether we can reuse the mechanism for multi-turn figure QA.

Your answer to the third question would change how we design multi-turn chart tasks, and the first would tell us where to instrument attention in our own vision tower.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
