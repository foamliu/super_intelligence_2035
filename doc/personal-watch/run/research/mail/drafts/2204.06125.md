Subject: unCLIP's prior/decoder split — when does the CLIP bottleneck pay off?

Dear Dr. Ramesh,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents and multimodal generation; my work is at https://foamliu.github.io/. Your two-stage formulation — a prior over CLIP image embeddings followed by a diffusion decoder — is the design we keep returning to when deciding whether a text-to-image stack should route through a frozen semantic embedding at all.

Three questions:

1. The prior is trained to match a CLIP ViT-H/14 image embedding, so the decoder never sees raw caption tokens. In your ablations, how much of the photorealism loss is attributable to that bottleneck versus to decoder capacity? We are weighing an embedding bottleneck against direct cross-attention for industrial imagery, where captions are short and repetitive.

2. You report the diffusion prior as more compute-efficient than the autoregressive one. Was that comparison at matched parameter count, matched sampling budget, or matched wall-clock? The answer decides whether we spend our prior budget on depth or on more diffusion steps.

3. Zero-shot language-guided editing is attributed to the joint embedding space. Does it survive when the decoder is retrained on a narrow domain, or does the prior have to be retrained too?

These answers would directly shape our architecture choice. Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
