Dear Dr. Srinivasan,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents and their evaluation harnesses (https://foamliu.github.io/). Your budget-constrained selection study is directly on point for us: a single full agent-harness comparison already costs more than the tuning we want to justify.

Three questions:

1. You report 27 of 35 Pareto steps as statistically unresolved. Which uncertainty estimate backs that claim — bootstrap over tasks, paired tests, or something else — and how did you pick the stopping point under a fixed budget?

2. Reported costs differ from token-based estimates by up to 11.7x. Is that gap driven mainly by cache hits, retries, or provider billing conventions? We intend to drive a budget controller from token counts, so which correction terms would you keep?

3. Successive halving reaches your target with 6.6% of exhaustive spend only with advance knowledge of task costs. Without that knowledge, would you stratify by task-type priors or run a small calibration batch first — and how much does that calibration erode the 6.6%?

The second answer decides whether our internal cost accounting is usable; the third whether we can adopt halving without a pilot phase.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
