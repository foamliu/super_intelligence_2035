## 正文

```
Dear Dr. Khattab,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents and their evaluation harnesses; my page is https://foamliu.github.io/.

What stayed with me in DSPy is that a pipeline should be compiled against a metric instead of hand-written: the reported 5-46% gap over expert-authored demonstrations is the part our own harness cannot reproduce, because every prompt is still curated by hand.

Three questions:
1. When the metric is noisy, for example an LLM judge with about 5% disagreement, does the compiler overfit the bootstrapped demonstrations? Is there a stopping rule you actually use?
2. In the 770M T5 result, how much of the gain comes from selected demonstrations versus updating module weights, and can the two be separated using the released artifacts?
3. With only around 200 labeled examples per task, is compiling still preferable to writing prompts, or does the approach need thousands?

Thank you for releasing the compiler and the case studies.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
```
