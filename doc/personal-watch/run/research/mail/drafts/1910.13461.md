```
Dear Professor Zettlemoyer,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents; you can find my work at https://foamliu.github.io/.

We are pretraining a ~2B model on a narrow technical corpus of chip-design documents and RTL, and BART is our reference for what a denoising objective adds beyond next-token prediction. Your ablation over the five noising functions -- token masking, token deletion, sentence permutation, document rotation, and text infilling with spans drawn from Poisson(lambda = 3) -- is the cleanest evidence we know on which corruption actually teaches generation.

Two questions:
1) If a decoder-only model could afford only one corruption this quarter, which do you expect to survive the move away from the encoder-decoder split: infilling, or plain 15% token masking? (We can run only one arm.)
2) In a narrow domain where masking cuts identifiers and syntax mid-token, did you observe the model learning to repair surface form instead of semantics, and would you spend the budget on syntax-aware spans instead?

Thank you for the paper; any pointer would directly shape our data pipeline.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
```
