## 正文

Dear Professor Alistarh,

I am Yang Liu, working on the BaiZe-ISEDA2027 (a ~2B pretrained model for chip design) and ZhuLong-DAC2027 (a closed-loop agent for storage/EDA experiments) projects. We are preparing the deployment path for BaiZe and keep coming back to your GPTQ paper: the one-shot, second-order view is what made 4-bit weights look like an engineering default rather than a research bet for us.

Three questions where your experience would change what we do:

1. At ~2B parameters, are there layer groups (first/last blocks, attention vs. MLP) where 4-bit GPTQ is consistently riskier, so that a mixed-precision fallback is worth the complexity?
2. Our calibration corpus is chip-design text and tool logs, not generic web text. Does the Hessian-based objective still hold under that shift, and is there a cheap way to detect calibration overfitting before we commit a checkpoint?
3. When KV cache rather than weights dominates memory in long tool traces, is there a practical rule for when 4-bit weights plus a smaller batch beats 8-bit?

Thank you for the paper and for making the code available.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
BaiZe-ISEDA2027 / ZhuLong-DAC2027
