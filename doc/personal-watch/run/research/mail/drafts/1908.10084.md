Sehr geehrter Herr Dr. Reimers,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents and evaluation; you can find my work at https://foamliu.github.io/.

Sentence-BERT is the baseline we keep returning to when building retrieval over long internal documents. The numbers in your abstract frame our design: with BERT, finding the most similar pair among 10,000 sentences takes about 50 million inference computations (~65 hours), while SBERT reduces this to about 5 seconds while keeping BERT-level accuracy.

Three questions:

1. Does the ~5 seconds include index construction, or only query encoding plus cosine search? At what corpus size does GPU-batched cross-encoding with approximate nearest neighbours close the gap again?
2. How much of the final STS score survives if the SNLI/MNLI stage is removed and only STS pairs are used? For a domain without entailment labels (hardware specifications), would you still recommend NLI pretraining on general data?
3. With mean pooling, how do you handle documents beyond the 512-token limit: truncation, chunk pooling, or chunk embeddings max-pooled? Which showed the smallest STS degradation?

Thank you for your time; answers would directly affect our embedding and chunking design.

Best regards,
Yang Liu
