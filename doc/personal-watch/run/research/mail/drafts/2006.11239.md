Subject: Diffusion in hindsight: three questions from a ~2B chip-design project

Dear Professor Abbeel,

I am Liu Yang, working on BaiZe-ISEDA2027 (a ~2B domain model for chip design) and ZhuLong-DAC2027 (an EDA agent harness). DDPM is the paper we return to when we need data from a distribution we can describe but not sample: rare waveform shapes in our vision line, and rare design situations for our benchmark.

Three questions:

1. Our vision task regresses continuous waveforms (period, rise time, duty cycle) rather than generating images. Is a diffusion objective over the signal itself worth its sampling cost compared with direct regression, and what would you measure first to decide?

2. We judge synthetic data by matching a handful of aggregate statistics against a small set of real samples. What is the cheapest check that a generated sample is faithful enough to train on, before trusting a model trained on it?

3. The weighted variational bound was derived for images. For 1-D signals whose dynamic range differs sharply across channels, would you keep the same weighting, or re-derive it from the signal's own noise schedule?

Thank you for the paper and the reference implementation.

Best regards,
Liu Yang
Super Intelligence 2035 / BaiZe-ISEDA2027 / ZhuLong-DAC2027
