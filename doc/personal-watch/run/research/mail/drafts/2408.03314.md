Dear Charlie,

I am Yang Liu, a researcher working on LLM agents and evaluation; you can find my work at https://foamliu.github.io/.

We are deciding how to spend a fixed inference budget on agentic tasks, so your framing matters to us: you evaluate both a dense process-based verifier and an adaptive update of the response distribution, show that the better mechanism depends on prompt difficulty, and report that a compute-optimal allocation improves efficiency by more than 4x over best-of-N.

Three questions:

1. How is prompt difficulty estimated at test time without labels: from the verifier's score distribution, a separate model, or rollouts? What share of the 4x gain comes from the difficulty estimate itself rather than from the allocation rule?
2. When verification is exact but expensive (compiling code, running tests), does the compute-optimal policy shift toward revision rather than verifier-guided search? What verifier-call to generation-call ratio did you use?
3. The FLOPs-matched result where test-time compute beats a 14x larger model: does it include verifier training compute, and does it hold on prompts where the smaller model's pass@1 is near zero?

Thank you for your time; answers would directly shape our inference-budget planner.
