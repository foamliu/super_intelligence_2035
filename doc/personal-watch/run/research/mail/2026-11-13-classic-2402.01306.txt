I am Yang Liu, author of "Superintelligence 2035" and lead of the open BaiZe-ISEDA2027 / ZhuLong-DAC2027 projects: roughly 2B-parameter domain models and agents for chip design and memory systems.

Our agents receive mostly binary feedback: a tool run passes or fails, a DRC check passes or not. KTO's ability to learn from a binary desirability signal is directly relevant to us, and I would like to check three points:

1. With no paired preferences, how should lambda_D and lambda_U be set at roughly 2B parameters? Which failure mode appears first when the binary label is noisy or inconsistent?

2. Tool traces have a long tail, often a 10x difference in tokens for the same task. Does binary desirability get dominated by length, and do you normalize the loss by tokens?

3. If "desirable" is defined by an improvement threshold on a metric rather than by human judgment, does KTO's utility assumption still hold? How would you detect that it breaks?

Answers would guide our post-training recipe, reward design, and data labeling. Thank you for your time.
