WaveNet's dilated causal convolution stack was my first clear reference for autoregressive modeling of long raw signals, and we now face a similar choice for waveform-like data.

Three specific questions:

1. The 2016 release uses a categorical head over 256 mu-law levels. When moving to higher-fidelity audio, how much of the quality gain comes from the output distribution (e.g., 16-bit categorical or mixture of logistics) versus from the dilated stack itself, at a matched compute budget?

2. The receptive field is set by the dilation cycle (1, 2, 4, ...) times the number of stacks. Under a fixed parameter and compute budget, is it better to add stacks (longer receptive field) or widen the residual/gate channels? Do you have a rule of thumb that survived later ablations?

3. For fast generation you cache the convolution queues. In practice, what dominates the per-sample cost: the gated activation unit or the memory traffic of the skip-connection sum? We need reproducible cost accounting for our ablation tables.
