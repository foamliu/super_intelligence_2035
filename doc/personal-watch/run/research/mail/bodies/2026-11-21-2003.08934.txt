We are assembling multimodal pretraining data for chip design, where synthetic multi-view images of packages and boards are one candidate source. Your NeRF paper is the reason we now treat view direction as part of the observation rather than as an augmentation nuisance.

1. Since the field is optimized per scene, I read your setting as deliberately single-scene. If the goal becomes one network that renders many unseen layouts, does the 5D coordinate interface still hold, or does it need an explicit scene-conditioning token?
2. Is there a practical rule for the number of positional-encoding bands relative to scene extent and camera baseline? Our scenes span roughly one order of magnitude in scale, and artifacts appear first at grazing views.
3. In the sparse-view, known-pose regime, which failure arrives first: floaters, or flattened geometry? That decides whether we spend budget on more views or on a depth prior.
