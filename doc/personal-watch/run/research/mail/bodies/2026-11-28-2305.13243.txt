We are building agent benchmarks for EDA flows, and your study is one of the few that reports what actually happens when an LLM drives a real design flow end to end.

Three questions, each of which would directly change how we design tasks and judges:

1. In your conversation loop, did the model see simulator or synthesis errors? Roughly what fraction of its Verilog failed at elaboration, at simulation, and at synthesis, and which error class dominated?

2. Who wrote the testbench and golden vectors? Would the design still count as correct if the model had authored its own checker, and how did you guard against that?

3. In hindsight, which part of the flow is best suited to a benchmark: spec-to-RTL, RTL bug fixing, or constraint/script generation? What is the cheapest signal that separates "compiles" from "functionally right"?
